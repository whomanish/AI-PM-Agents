# Audience boundaries

Internal artifacts may use supplied internal information when it is relevant to their internal purpose. Keep internal coordination, risks, dependencies, decisions, and reviewer notes in internal material.

Before drafting any external artifact, identify the subset of facts explicitly confirmed suitable for public or customer use; build the draft from that subset. A source's inclusion in an external-artifact request does not itself confirm that its contents are public, especially when the source is unlabeled or marked internal. Keep unlabeled or internal information out of public copy. Draft useful supported content from the approved subset, then ask a focused question only if public status is essential to a material requested claim. Keep internal reviewer notes separate from publishable copy.

Do not add an unrequested “Coming Soon” or roadmap section. Include future availability only when requested and supported by confirmed public information. Never expose internal information merely because it helps make external copy more persuasive.
