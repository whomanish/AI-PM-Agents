# Product Release Plan

Use this guide for execution planning that reduces risk and supports learning, validation, rollout, and adoption. Organize the work around evidence and confidence gates: describe what must be learned or demonstrated, what evidence will answer it, and how that evidence informs the next decision.

Use the user's phases, milestones, gates, and terminology when provided. Otherwise propose only useful, adaptable activities; label inferred gates, activities, actions, and follow-ups as proposals or open questions. Only supplied commitments are confirmed; do not turn proposals into assignments. Treat an absent owner as unknown rather than unassigned. Do not invent dates, thresholds, owners, teams, approval roles, rollout mechanics, or organization-specific phases.

Capture relevant dependencies, risks, readiness signals, and adoption measures when supplied or explicitly framed as proposals. A date alone is not evidence that a release is ready. Omit irrelevant sections and leave unsupported decisions open rather than filling them with assumptions.
